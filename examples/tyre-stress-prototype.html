Tyre stress, before tyre wear

Can a load, motion and slip index distinguish gentle driving, sliding, burnouts and unloaded wheels?

This is an uncalibrated stress index. It does not measure rubber loss, remaining tread, grip loss or tyre life.

Model assumptions

rate = load_kN × max(car_speed_m/s, |wheel_rad/s| × nominal_radius_m) × SlipFraction / 255

Integrate only while both ends of a sample interval have ground contact and positive load. Temperature is displayed, with no invented wear multiplier.

Nominal radius:

0.300 m is an illustrative default, not a measured radius. Set all four for the car being compared. W0–W3 are packet indices; corner mapping needs live validation.

Read this before comparing runs

SlipFraction is relative to the slip producing peak force. Values 0–254 approach that slip; 255 means beyond it and cannot measure how far beyond. This index deliberately inherits that saturation.

The motion term uses the car reference-point speed and a nominal wheel radius. It is a proxy, not per-wheel ground speed or measured slip power. Displayed force fields are reserved for investigating a later energy model.

Each segment starts at zero. Recorded OutSim gaps, identity changes, duplicate/backwards clocks, and source or receipt intervals over 250 ms start another segment. No stress is inferred across those boundaries. Missing data can therefore undercount stress.

Make one recording per known tyre stint. This OutSim/OutGauge report cannot detect fresh tyres, a same-car driver change, or every teleport. It cannot reconstruct wear from before recording started. A completed file does not certify a complete stint.

Compare the same car, compound, track, setup and radius assumptions first. Internal tyre air temperature is neither ambient nor tread surface temperature. Unknown temperature effects and local flat spots remain unmodeled.
